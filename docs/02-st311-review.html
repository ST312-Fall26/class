Degrees of freedom

The Wardrobe Analogy

Imagine you have 7 different shirts to wear over a 7-day week. On Monday, you have 7 choices. On Tuesday, 6 choices. By the time Sunday rolls around, you only have 1 shirt left. You have no choice on Sunday—your decision was perfectly determined by the previous 6 days. You had 6 days of true flexibility, meaning your degrees of freedom are 6.

Degrees of freedom

Note: We need degrees of freedom because in a single mean test, we also have to use data to estimate \(s\) for the standard error. In a single proportion test, the standard error is entirely estimated using our statistic \(\hat{p}\). We will re-visit this when we review single proportion.

How many independent pieces of information you have

If you have \(\bar{x}\) and n = 50…. the 50th observation is “locked” into whatever the exact number makes the mean equal to \(\bar{x}\) (2035).

We are working with 49 degrees of freedom!

t vs z-test

\[{SE(\bar{x})} = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\]

\[\text{Sample Standard Deviation } (s) = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}\]

vs

\[SE({\hat{p})} = \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}\]

\[{SE(p_o)} = \sqrt{\frac{p_0(1 - p_0)}{n}}\]

Because we know the standard error when we calculate our statistic \(\hat{p}\), we are justified to do a z-test and a standard normal distribution.

Other formulas

\[z = \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{\frac{p_0(1 - p_0)}{n}}}\]

\[\hat{p} \pm z^* \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}}\]

Assumptions

Hypothesis test:

\[n p_0 \ge 10 \quad \text{and} \quad n (1 - p_0) \ge 10\]

Confidence interval:

\[n \hat{p} \ge 10 \quad \text{and} \quad n (1 - \hat{p}) \ge 10\]

z-statistic

\[z = \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{\frac{p_0(1 - p_0)}{n}}}\]

\[z = \frac{0.70 - 0.50}{\sqrt{\frac{0.50(1 - 0.50)}{50}}}\]

\[z = \frac{0.20}{\sqrt{\frac{0.25}{50}}}\]

\[z = \frac{0.20}{\sqrt{0.005}}\]

\[z = \frac{0.20}{0.07071}\]

\[z \approx 2.83\]

Draw out how the p-value would be calculated.
