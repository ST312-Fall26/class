Probability definitions

– An event is any collection of results or outcomes of a procedure

– The sample space for a procedure consists of all possible events (denoted as S = {})

– Events are usually labeled with letters (A, B, C, etc.)

– P(A) is the notation for the probability of event A

– \(P(A^c)\) is the complement of A. You can think of the \(^c\) as the word NOT

More practice

A company has two types of employees: 60% are experienced and 40% are new. An experienced employee has a 90% chance of completing a project on time, while a new employee has a 60% chance.

You randomly select an employee who completed their project on time. What is the probability that the employee is experienced?

What do we know? And what do we need to find out?

What we know

\(P(E)=0.60\)

\(P(E^c)=0.40\)

\(P(O\mid E)=0.90\)

\(P(O\mid E^c)=0.60\)

What are we trying to solve?

What we want

\(P(E\mid O)\)

We want to find the probability that the employee is experienced given that they completed the project on time.

So what else do we need?

What we need

We need to find:

\(P(O)\)

Then we can use Bayes’ Rule:

\(P(E\mid O)=\frac{P(O\mid E)P(E)}{P(O)}\)

How do we find \(P(O)\)?

P(O)

If I want the probability of O, I want the probability when O and E occur, and the probability when O and \(E^c\) occur….

Let’s think back to this rule: P(A|B) = \(\frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}\)

P(O|E) = \(\frac{P(O \cap E)}{P(E)}\)

How can we rewrite this?

P(O)

\(P(O | E) = \frac{P(O \cap E)}{P(E)}\)

\(P(O\cap E)=P(O\mid E)P(E)\)

and

\(P(O| E^c) = \frac{P(O \cap E^c)}{P(E^c)}\)

\(P(O\cap E^c)=P(O\mid E^c)P(E^c)\)

and we should add them together to get \(P(O)\)!

Think back

\(P(E)=0.60\)

\(P(E^c)=0.40\)

\(P(O\mid E)=0.90\)

\(P(O\mid E^c)=0.60\)

\[ P(O) = (0.90)(0.60) + (0.60)(0.40) = 0.78 \]

and now we can answer the question!

Solutions

\[ P(E \mid O) = \frac{P(O \mid E)P(E)}{P(O)} \]

\[ P(O) = (0.90)(0.60) + (0.60)(0.40) \]

\[ P(O) = 0.54 + 0.24 = 0.78 \]

\[ P(E \mid O) = \frac{(0.90)(0.60)}{0.78} \]

\[ P(E \mid O) = \frac{0.54}{0.78} \approx 0.6923 \]

\[ \boxed{P(E \mid O) \approx 69.23\%} \]

Independence

Events are independent if the outcome of one event is not affected by the outcome of the other

We go from: \(P(A \cap B)\) = P(A|B)*P(B)

To: \(P(A \cap B)\) = P(A) * P(B)
